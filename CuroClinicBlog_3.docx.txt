What Is PCOS?
PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome) is a hormonal disorder in which the ovaries may produce higher-than-normal levels of male hormones called androgens. This hormonal imbalance can affect normal ovulation and may lead to irregular periods, difficulty conceiving, and other symptoms.
The condition may also involve the presence of multiple small fluid-filled follicles in the ovaries. However, not every woman with PCOS has ovarian cysts, and the symptoms can vary from person to person.
Common Symptoms of PCOS Every Woman Should Know
Recognizing the early signs of PCOS can help women seek timely medical advice. Common symptoms include:
Irregular Periods
One of the most common signs of PCOS is irregular menstrual cycles. Women may experience delayed periods, missed periods, or fewer menstrual cycles in a year due to irregular ovulation.
Excess Hair Growth
Increased androgen levels may cause unwanted hair growth on the face, chin, chest, or other areas of the body. This condition is known as hirsutism.
Acne and Oily Skin
Hormonal changes associated with PCOS can increase oil production, leading to persistent acne, oily skin, and skin breakouts even beyond teenage years.
Weight Gain or Difficulty Losing Weight
Many women with PCOS experience weight gain, especially around the abdomen. Hormonal imbalance and insulin resistance can make weight management more challenging.
Hair Thinning or Hair Loss
Some women may notice thinning of scalp hair due to increased androgen activity, which can affect hair growth patterns.
Fertility Difficulties
Since PCOS can affect ovulation, some women may experience difficulty becoming pregnant. Proper diagnosis and treatment can improve fertility outcomes.
Darkened Skin Patches
Some women with PCOS develop dark, thickened skin patches, especially around the neck, underarms, or other body folds. This may be associated with insulin resistance.
What Causes PCOS?
The exact cause of PCOS is not completely understood, but several factors may contribute to its development.
Hormonal Imbalance
Higher levels of androgens can interfere with normal egg development and ovulation.
Insulin Resistance
Many women with PCOS have insulin resistance, where the body does not respond effectively to insulin. This can increase insulin levels and influence hormone production.
Genetics
Women with a family history of PCOS may have a higher chance of developing the condition.
Lifestyle Factors
Poor diet, lack of physical activity, stress, and unhealthy lifestyle habits may worsen PCOS symptoms.
How Is PCOS Diagnosed?
PCOS diagnosis involves a detailed evaluation by a gynecologist. At Curo Clinics, Kokapet, Dr. Sivaharika Rayudu evaluates symptoms, medical history, and overall health before recommending suitable investigations.
Diagnosis may include:
Menstrual cycle assessment
Physical examination
Blood tests to check hormone levels
Ultrasound examination of the ovaries
Evaluation of weight, metabolism, and related health factors
A proper diagnosis helps create a personalized treatment plan based on individual needs.
Treatment Options for PCOS
PCOS management focuses on controlling symptoms, improving hormonal balance, and supporting long-term health.
Lifestyle Modifications
A healthy lifestyle plays an important role in managing PCOS. Balanced nutrition, regular exercise, adequate sleep, and stress management can help improve symptoms.
Weight Management Support
Maintaining a healthy weight can support better hormone regulation and improve insulin sensitivity in many women with PCOS.
Medications
Depending on symptoms, doctors may recommend medications to regulate periods, manage acne or excess hair growth, improve insulin sensitivity, or support fertility.
Fertility Support
For women planning pregnancy, appropriate medical guidance can help improve ovulation and increase the chances of conception.
Can PCOS Be Prevented?
PCOS cannot always be completely prevented, especially when genetic factors are involved. However, maintaining healthy habits can help reduce symptoms and improve overall reproductive health.
Helpful steps include:
Eating a balanced diet
Maintaining a healthy weight
Exercising regularly
Managing stress effectively
Scheduling regular gynecological check-ups
Monitoring menstrual changes
When Should You Consult a Gynecologist?
Women should consider consulting a gynecologist if they experience:
Irregular or missed periods
Sudden weight changes
Excess facial hair growth
Persistent acne
Difficulty getting pregnant
Unusual menstrual symptoms
Early consultation helps identify hormonal concerns and allows timely management.
